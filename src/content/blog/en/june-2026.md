## Floods and Drought

I read an article in the National Trust monthly magazine about the Charlecote Park in Warwickshire (UK). In January 2024, the Tudor mansion flooded multiple times when the River Avon burst its banks during the Storm Henk.

In March 2025, the natural spring that they used for water dried out for the first time in 100 years. The groundwater level has fallen and the sprint won’t be a reliable source of water anymore.

The positive news is that institutions like the National Trust, but also local communities, are not defaulting to natural based solutions instead of the usual eye-sore high-carbon concrete-based flood defences. They call this process natural flood management, usually based on digging ponds, restoring marshes and creating ”leaky dams”.

I recently looked into SuDS (Sustainable Drainage System), a way to divert surface water with soakaways in parks, permeable paving on driveways, green roofs and, my favourite, depaving actions.

I’m currently starting a SuDS journey in my property. This month I started with the  easiest lower-hanging fruit and I installed two water butts, for a total of 500 litres of water storage. This winter, I’ll tackle the bigger job, depaving the back garden by the massive slab of concrete that the previous owner laid down.
